## 1. Shape of the spec, in one paragraph

A pure `StockItem` aggregate with a `Reservation` child entity and a pure domain service that makes reservation all-or-nothing across an order's lines and builds the three facts; **one** `BackgroundService` subscribing to the five `fulfillment.stock.*` subjects over raw NATS, dispatching through the existing `src/Cqrs` dispatcher, **handling requests concurrently with a bounded degree and one DI scope each**; one short `ReadCommitted` transaction per command that locks the order's stock rows **one statement at a time, in an application-fixed order, under `WITH (UPDLOCK, HOLDLOCK, ROWLOCK)`** before anything else — which is what makes two concurrent reserves for the last units resolve to exactly one `stock.reserved.v1` and one `stock.rejected.v1`, and what makes the `orderReference`-scoped responder idempotency race-free under `READ_COMMITTED_SNAPSHOT`; facts leave through Fulfillment's own copies of the outbox writer, relay and Kafka publisher to `otc.fulfillment.facts.v1`. One bounded Orders change goes first: the `x-correlation-id`/`x-request-id` headers, without which Fulfillment cannot stamp `correlationId = orderId`. **No migration, no new package.** The live first boot is designed, not discovered: the four parked `stock.reserve` commands resume unattended, and `orders.create` becomes genuinely end-to-end for the first time.

## Gate ruling (2026-09-04)

**Both gate rows APPROVED AS RECOMMENDED. No row reopened, no row amended.**

**Row 1 — the idempotent-consumer copy.** Approved: **do not copy it into Fulfillment.** #8's parity guard requires a copy only from a service with **both** a `processed_events` configuration **and** a Kafka consumer `BackgroundService` — verified directly in `tests/Orders.UnitTests/IdempotentConsumerParityTests.cs`, which chains `.Where(HasRelationalProcessedEventsConfiguration)` then `.Where(HasKafkaConsumerBackgroundService)`. Fulfillment has the first and, per `saga.md` §5, will never have the second. The divergence from #7 is deliberate and is to be recorded as such, so a reviewer comparing the two runs reads it as a decision rather than an omission.

**Row 2 — concurrent request handling (`FS18`).** Approved as recommended: concurrent handling, `SemaphoreSlim(32)`, one DI scope per request, in-flight tasks awaited on shutdown. Verified at the gate: `src/Orders/Presentation/OrdersCreateResponder.cs:48` is `await HandleAsync(...)` inside the subscription loop — genuinely sequential — and its own remark at line 36 already anticipates this feature (*"Revisit if a later feature adds a second concurrent RPC subject"*). The decisive evidence is the one recorded in the row: **two serialised reserves also yield one `accepted` and one `rejected`**, so this feature's own acceptance criterion would be green while proving nothing about the lock protocol. `tasks.md` G11's arming — expecting `FS6` to **stay green** under the reversion — is the point of the requirement, not a formality.

**On the ported-idiom ledger, which this feature is the first to carry.** Twelve rows, each with a guard named in `tasks.md`. Four are properties that would otherwise have been silent losses, and the fourth is the strongest evidence yet that the convention earns its cost: **banning `CONFLICT` from this service's error mapper, because feature 42 made that code terminal — a deadlock victim answered `CONFLICT` would end the order's saga while satisfying `R32`'s text exactly.** That is a cross-feature interaction no requirement mentions, found by asking what supplies a property rather than whether a requirement is met.
