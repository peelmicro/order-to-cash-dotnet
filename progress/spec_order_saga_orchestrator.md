## 1. Shape of the spec, in one paragraph

The orchestrator is **one Kafka `BackgroundService`** (group `orders.saga`, `AutoOffsetReset.Earliest`, three topics, one serial consume loop) behind an `IFactStreamSubscriber` port whose single adapter is the only type in the repository touching `Confluent.Kafka`'s consumer API; it parses the envelope, routes on `eventType` through a **fourteen-row** declarative step table (four skips, ten consumed facts) and dispatches an explicit per-fact command on the **existing** in-process dispatcher; each fact `ICommandHandler` is the whole transactional unit over the **existing, unmodified** `IdempotentConsumer`, committing the dedup record, the aggregate change, the outbox rows and a durable **pending-command row** in one transaction; the owed NATS command is issued only **after** commit, through a post-commit `IDispatcher.PublishAsync` → five `IEventHandler` classes (`OrderSagas`, #7's `@Saga` role) → a bounded in-process channel → a dispatch `BackgroundService` that runs SO4's 3 × 5 s / 500 ms-doubling retry **off the consume loop**; exhausted commands **park** in `saga_commands` and a sweeper retries them on capped backoff indefinitely — the in-process hop is the fast path, the durable row plus sweeper is the guarantee. **No migration** (both tables shipped in phase 6). **No new package.** Integration proof runs against stand-in NATS responders with real Kafka, real NATS and real MS-SQL.

## 5. Gate ruling (2026-09-04)

**Both gate rows APPROVED AS RECOMMENDED. No row reopened, no row amended.** The spec stands exactly as written; this section records the ruling and the evidence gathered answering the human's one question at the gate.

**Row 5 — the Kafka confinement rule.** Approved: narrow `FactPublisherConfinementTests` to the four producer type prefixes, and add `FactConsumerConfinementTests` confining the four consumer prefixes to `*.Infrastructure.Messaging.Consumers`. Both armed. `tasks.md` group J is unblocked and lands with F1.

**Row 8 — what plays #7's `@Saga` role.** Approved: post-commit event → five `IEventHandler` classes in `OrderSagas.cs` → bounded `Channel<T>` (`DropWrite`) → `SagaCommandDispatchWorker : BackgroundService`. The durable `saga_commands` row plus the sweeper remains the only guarantee.

### What the human asked, and what the #7 checkout answered

*"Is this a .NET issue, or did #7 have it too?"* — asked before ruling, and the answer changes how both rows should be read.

**Row 5 is a #8-only artifact, not a stack difference.** #7's `eslint.config.mjs` restricts `kafkajs` **only as a domain-purity rule**, scoped to `apps/*/src/domain/**` — it never confined Kafka to the outbox, so a consumer in `infrastructure/` was always legal there and #7 had no rule to change and no gate to hold. The collision exists because #8's own guard (added in feature 14) is **broader than the requirement it cites**: R14 forbids handlers, aggregates and domain services from *publishing*, and the guard forbids *any* dependency on the library outside one namespace. The approved change repairs an over-written guard rather than relaxing a correct one, and leaves the repository with one more armed rule than either assessment has today.

**Row 8 is the same problem #7 had, and #7's own design says so in the past tense.** `specs/order_saga_orchestrator/design.md` §3.1 in the #7 checkout: *"The NATS retry budget **no longer** sits on the consumer thread … a slow responder for order A no longer delays a fact for order B."* Its pre-gate design **did** put the budget on the consume loop and justified the ≈16.5 s worst case as *"deliberately under the 30 s Kafka session timeout (no heartbeats during a slow handler ⇒ rebalance loop otherwise)"*. It moved off the loop only in its **post-gate revision**, and as a *side effect* of the human overruling an unrelated question (whether to adopt `@nestjs/cqrs`) — #7 acquired the property largely by accident.

**Where .NET differs is the forcing function, not the problem.** kafkajs heartbeats *between* messages, so a blocking retry budget costs #7 a group rebalance — a loud failure. librdkafka heartbeats from its own background thread, so the identical mistake in #8 costs nothing observable: slow, correct, silent. That is the failure shape this repository keeps paying for, and it is the whole reason row 8 was gated rather than footnoted.

**And #7's mechanism was not free.** `apps/orders/src/application/sagas/order.sagas.ts`'s own header records that a saga stream which throws **terminates its subscription to the global event stream silently**, stopping that saga from dispatching another command *for the lifetime of the process* — defended with a `catchError` resubscribe recipe and, ultimately, by the sweeper. The framework construct traded an explicit queue for an implicit failure mode. #8's channel-plus-worker has no equivalent hazard and must be written by hand; both keep the durable row and sweeper as the sole guarantee.
